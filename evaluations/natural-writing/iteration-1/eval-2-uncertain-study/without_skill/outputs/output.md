A voluntary four-week pilot at one clinic offers an early reason to evaluate the scheduling tool further. Among 18 participating nurses, median weekly scheduling time fell from 46 to 39 minutes. Without a control group, however, that change cannot be attributed to the tool; concurrent staffing changes may explain part of it. Six of the nine nurses who returned the optional survey preferred the tool, but the responses do not establish a preference among all participants or nurses elsewhere. Patient safety was not measured. Before expanding adoption, run a longer evaluation with a comparison group, document staffing changes, and collect feedback from more participants. Include patient safety measures so the next decision considers more than scheduling time and reported preference.
