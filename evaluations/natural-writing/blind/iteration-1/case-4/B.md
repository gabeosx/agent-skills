Replication is not a backup. It copies changes to another instance, including accidental deletions. A replica may therefore contain the same unwanted changes as the source. Replication can improve availability, but it does not by itself provide point-in-time recovery.

A backup preserves an earlier state that can be restored, subject to its retention policy. Its recovery value depends on which states remain available under that policy. For this system, point-in-time recovery requires both a base backup and an unbroken sequence of write-ahead logs covering the required recovery interval. A gap in that sequence prevents replay through the gap. Plan replication and backups around their separate purposes: maintaining availability and restoring retained data states. Neither configuration warrants a promise of zero downtime or zero data loss.
