A voluntary four-week pilot at one clinic offers an encouraging but limited signal for the scheduling tool. Among 18 participating nurses, median weekly scheduling time fell from 46 to 39 minutes. Without a control group, that change cannot be attributed to the tool; concurrent staffing changes may have contributed. Nine nurses completed the optional survey, and six preferred the tool, so the responses describe only half the participants and may reflect response bias. The pilot did not measure patient safety and cannot establish safety effects or performance across other clinics. A reasonable next step is a longer, controlled evaluation across several clinics, with staffing changes tracked and scheduling time, user experience, and patient safety assessed using predefined measures.
