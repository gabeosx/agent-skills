A voluntary four-week pilot at one clinic offers a reason to investigate the scheduling tool further. Among 18 participating nurses, median weekly scheduling time fell from 46 to 39 minutes. With no control group and staffing changes occurring during the pilot, the study cannot establish how much, if any, of that reduction the tool caused. Six of the nine nurses who returned the optional survey preferred the tool; that result may not reflect the views of all participants or nurses elsewhere. Patient safety was not measured. Before broader adoption, run a longer evaluation with a comparison group, track staffing changes, seek feedback from all participants, and include patient safety measures alongside scheduling time.
