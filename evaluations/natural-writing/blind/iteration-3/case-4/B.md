Replication and backups serve different recovery needs. Replication copies changes to another instance, including accidental deletions. It can improve availability, but a replicated instance may contain the same unwanted changes as the source. Replication is therefore not a substitute for a backup.

A backup preserves an earlier state that can be restored, subject to its retention policy. For this system, point-in-time recovery requires both a base backup and an unbroken sequence of write-ahead logs covering the recovery interval. Replication alone does not provide those recovery materials. Maintain backups and the required logs alongside replication, and select retention policies that support the recovery points you need. Neither mechanism warrants a promise of zero downtime or zero data loss.
