A voluntary four-week pilot at one clinic offers a reason to evaluate the scheduling tool further. Among the 18 nurses who used it, median weekly scheduling time fell from 46 to 39 minutes. Without a control group, that change cannot be attributed to the tool; concurrent staffing changes may explain part of it. Nine nurses completed the optional survey, and six of those nine preferred the tool. That response does not establish a preference among all participants or nurses elsewhere. The pilot did not measure patient safety. A next step would be a longer evaluation with a comparison group, tracking staffing changes and scheduling time while separately assessing safety before deciding whether to expand use.
