A voluntary four-week pilot at one clinic offers an initial reason to evaluate the scheduling tool further. Among 18 participating nurses, median weekly scheduling time fell from 46 to 39 minutes. The pilot cannot establish that the tool caused the reduction: there was no control group, and concurrent staffing changes may explain part of it. Six of the nine nurses who returned the optional survey preferred the tool, but that response does not establish a preference among all participants or nurses elsewhere. Patient safety was not measured. Before considering wider adoption, run a longer evaluation with a comparable control group, track staffing changes, seek feedback from all participants, and include patient safety measures alongside scheduling time.
