# Decision on Harbor for next quarter

Approve Harbor for a one-quarter pilot in the food-delivery program, conditional on a checked route mapping, a successful export rehearsal and a named administrator before launch. Other programs should retain their current calendars. The evaluation supports a limited trial, with broader adoption dependent on evidence gathered during the quarter.

Today, three coordinators maintain separate calendars and reconcile availability by email. They spent a combined 11 hours reconciling changes during a two-week observation period. That establishes a current workload, but does not establish annual savings or how much time Harbor would save.

In the two-week Harbor trial, 24 volunteers and two coordinators participated. Nineteen volunteers accepted at least one shift through the link; five booked by phone, with a coordinator entering their shifts. The record does not explain why those five used the phone. Harbor let one coordinator see conflicting assignments across the two test routes, while phone booking and data entry remained necessary. Coordinator time savings were not measured.

The first week's CSV import assigned four volunteers to the wrong route because old route names were inconsistent. The team corrected the mapping and re-imported; no wrong-route assignment remained after the second import. Checking that mapping is a prerequisite for the pilot.

Harbor would cost $90 per month for this program; the trial was free. A shared spreadsheet with protected route columns would avoid a new subscription cost and accommodate phone bookings, but coordinators would still check conflicts manually. A one-week spreadsheet exercise found two conflicts during review. There is no comparable Harbor conflict-rate measurement. Keeping separate calendars would avoid migration and subscription costs while retaining email reconciliation and no combined conflict view.

Before launch, rehearse exporting Harbor's CSV and rebuilding schedules elsewhere, including phone-entered bookings. The interface offers an export, but reversibility has not yet been demonstrated. The program director must also name an administrator to maintain route mappings, enter phone bookings and handle volunteer access questions; nobody has volunteered.

At quarter end, review coordinator time, conflicts found, volunteer booking methods and administrative workload to decide whether to stop, extend or expand. Numerical success thresholds still need agreement. The cost and suitability of moving all programs remain unevaluated.
