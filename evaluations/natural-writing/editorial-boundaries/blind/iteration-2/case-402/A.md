A voluntary four-week pilot at one clinic found that median weekly scheduling time among 18 nurses fell from 46 to 39 minutes. This seven-minute reduction is worth investigating, but the pilot cannot establish that the scheduling tool caused it: there was no control group, and concurrent staffing changes may explain part of the difference. Nine nurses returned the optional survey, of whom six preferred the tool. That response offers limited feedback and cannot establish preferences among all participants or nurses elsewhere. Patient safety was not measured. Before considering wider adoption, run a controlled evaluation that accounts for staffing changes, seeks feedback from all participants and measures patient safety alongside scheduling time.
