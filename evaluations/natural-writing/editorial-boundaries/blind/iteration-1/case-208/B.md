Approve a six-week extension of the Lantern warehouse-routing pilot at a cost of up to $9,000, subject to completion and approval of the safety review before the extension starts. The extension would test a routing change for parcels requiring a manual address check, referred to within the project as the “blue lane.” This term does not identify a carrier or service level.

Lantern operates at two warehouses. During the initial four-week pilot, 260 parcels requiring manual address checks had a median dwell time of 31 hours. For comparison, 240 such parcels processed in the prior month had a median dwell time of 34 hours. Damage reports numbered four during the pilot and three in the prior month.

These results do not establish that routing caused a change in either dwell time or damage. The cohorts were not randomized, and the pilot month included fewer international parcels. That difference in parcel mix limits the comparison: the observed three-hour reduction cannot yet be treated as an effect of the routing approach. The damage counts likewise do not support a conclusion about the change's safety.

The proposed extension would track parcel mix, document the workload involved in manual review and compare more similar cohorts. These measures would give the board a better basis for assessing the routing change and its operational demands. They would not, on their own, remove every source of uncertainty.

The extension costs $1,500 per week for six weeks and uses existing staff. Approval would authorize only this capped extension; permanent expansion and new hires would require a separate decision. The safety review is still incomplete. The board is asked to approve the extension subject to that review being completed and approved before work begins.
