Approve a six-week extension of Lantern, the routing pilot at two warehouses, with spending capped at $9,000 and a condition that the safety review be completed and approved before the extension starts. The extension would test a routing change for parcels requiring a manual address check, known within the project as the “blue lane.” This label describes an address-review requirement, not a carrier or service level.

The initial four-week pilot offers a reason to investigate further, but does not establish that the routing change improved results. Among 260 parcels requiring manual address checks, median dwell time was 31 hours. For 240 such parcels processed in the prior month, the median was 34 hours. The cohorts were not randomized, and the pilot month had fewer international parcels. Differences in parcel mix may account for some or all of the observed difference. Damage reports numbered four during the pilot and three in the prior-month cohort; these data establish no routing effect on damage either.

The proposed extension costs $1,500 per week for six weeks and uses existing staff. It would track parcel mix, document the workload created by manual review and compare more similar cohorts. These measurements would help assess whether the routing change merits further consideration and what it requires of warehouse staff. They would also give reviewers a firmer basis for interpreting dwell-time comparisons than the initial before-and-after figures provide.

Safety approval remains outstanding. Approval of this proposal would authorize only the capped extension, subject to that approval; it would not authorize permanent expansion or new hires. The board is asked to decide whether the opportunity to gather better evidence warrants up to $9,000 for this six-week test under those conditions.
