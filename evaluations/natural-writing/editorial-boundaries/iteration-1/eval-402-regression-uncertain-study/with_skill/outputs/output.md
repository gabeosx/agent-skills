In a voluntary four-week pilot at one clinic, 18 nurses used a scheduling tool, and median weekly scheduling time fell from 46 to 39 minutes. Without a control group, the study cannot establish that the tool caused the decrease; concurrent staffing changes may explain part of it. Nine nurses completed the optional survey, and six preferred the tool. That response does not establish a preference among all participants or nurses elsewhere. Patient safety was not measured. A next step is a controlled pilot that tracks staffing changes, seeks feedback from all participants and measures patient safety alongside scheduling time before any decision about wider adoption.
