In a voluntary four-week pilot at one clinic, 18 nurses used a scheduling tool, and median weekly scheduling time fell from 46 to 39 minutes. Without a control group, the seven-minute reduction cannot be attributed to the tool; concurrent staffing changes may explain part of it. Nine nurses completed the optional survey, and six of those nine preferred the tool. That response describes the respondents, not necessarily all participants or nurses elsewhere. The pilot did not measure patient safety. A next step is a controlled evaluation that tracks staffing changes, seeks feedback from all participants and includes patient-safety measures before deciding whether to expand use.
