In a voluntary four-week pilot at one clinic, 18 nurses used a scheduling tool, and median weekly scheduling time fell from 46 to 39 minutes. The observed seven-minute reduction warrants further evaluation, but the study had no control group and concurrent staffing changes may explain part of it. Nine nurses completed the optional survey; six of those nine preferred the tool. Those responses describe half the participants and cannot establish preference across the full group or other clinics. Patient safety was not measured. A next step is a controlled evaluation that tracks staffing changes, collects feedback from more participants and measures patient safety alongside scheduling time before making a broader rollout decision.
