Approve a six-week extension of Lantern, the routing pilot at two warehouses, with spending capped at $9,000 and a start conditional on completion and approval of the safety review. The extension would test a routing change for parcels requiring a manual address check, known within the project as “blue lane” parcels. This term does not identify a carrier or service level.

The initial four-week pilot provides a reason to investigate further, but it does not establish that routing improved outcomes. Among 260 parcels requiring manual address checks, median dwell time was 31 hours. The comparable figure for 240 parcels processed the prior month was 34 hours. These cohorts were not randomized, and the pilot month included fewer international parcels. Differences in parcel mix may account for some or all of the observed difference. Damage reports numbered four during the pilot and three in the prior-month cohort; the data do not establish a routing effect on damage either.

The extension would cost $1,500 per week for six weeks and use existing staff. Its purpose is to obtain more useful evidence by tracking parcel mix, documenting manual-review workload and comparing more similar cohorts. These measures would help the board assess whether any observed dwell-time differences persist when the comparison better accounts for the parcels being handled, and what the routing change requires of staff.

The safety review remains incomplete. Approval of the extension must not permit work to begin before that review is completed and approved. The requested authorization covers only this capped, six-week test; it does not authorize permanent expansion or new hires. The board is asked to approve the extension on those terms so the team can evaluate the proposed routing change before seeking any broader commitment.
