# Decision: a limited Harbor pilot next quarter

Approve Harbor for a one-quarter pilot in the food-delivery program, conditional on a successful export rehearsal, a checked route mapping, and a named administrator before launch. Other programs should retain their current calendars. The evaluation supports testing Harbor further within this scope; it does not establish the cost or suitability of adopting it across all programs.

Three coordinators currently maintain separate calendars and reconcile availability by email. They spent a combined 11 hours reconciling changes during a two-week observation period. That is evidence of current work, not an estimate of annual savings. The Harbor trial did not measure coordinator time saved.

During the two-week trial, 24 volunteers and two coordinators used Harbor. Nineteen volunteers accepted at least one shift through the link. Five used phone calls, with a coordinator entering their shifts; the record does not explain their choice. Harbor let one coordinator see conflicting assignments across the two test routes, but phone bookings and their manual entry remained necessary.

The first week's CSV import assigned four volunteers to the wrong route because the old route names were inconsistent. The team corrected the mapping and re-imported; no wrong-route assignment remained afterward. Checking route mappings before the pilot is essential, and maintaining them must be part of the administrator's work.

Harbor would cost $90 per month for this pilot, following a free trial. A shared spreadsheet with protected route columns has no new subscription cost and accommodates phone bookings, but requires manual conflict checks. Its one-week exercise found two conflicts during manual review; without comparable Harbor measurements, this does not establish which approach has a lower conflict rate. Keeping separate calendars avoids migration and subscription costs while retaining email reconciliation and no combined conflict view.

Before launch, rehearse exporting Harbor's CSV and rebuilding schedules elsewhere, including phone-entered bookings. The interface offers CSV export, but reversibility has not yet been demonstrated. The program director must also name an administrator to maintain mappings, enter phone bookings, and handle access questions; nobody has volunteered.

At quarter-end, review coordinator time, conflicts found, booking methods, and administrative workload before deciding whether to stop, extend, or expand. Managers still need to agree numerical success thresholds.
