# Pattern catalog

Editorial heuristics reviewed 2026-10-23. These are ways to diagnose a passage,
not authorship tests. “Priority” describes editing usefulness, not a statistical
confidence estimate. Read the surrounding argument before changing an example.

| Family / priority | Look for | Better decision | Keep it when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unneeded corrective contrast / high | “Not just X, but Y”; “It isn't X. It's Y.” | State the actual claim; develop its reason. | X is a live misconception, an alternative under consideration, or a precise technical distinction. |
| Significance without substance / high | “underscores the importance”; “a pivotal shift”; “a testament to” | Describe the supported consequence, mechanism, or change. | The significance is explained and relevant; a quotation needs exact wording. |
| Vague authority / high | “experts agree”; “research shows”; “widely regarded” | Keep a real attribution attached to its actual claim. Remove unsupported appeals to authority. | A source is supplied and the attribution accurately describes its findings. |
| Generic promotion / high | “seamless, robust, powerful”; “revolutionize your workflow” | Describe what the product does and the conditions or limits. | A precise technical meaning or supported claim requires the term. “Robust regression” stays. |
| Assistant residue / high | “Certainly!”; “Here is a comprehensive overview”; “I hope this helps” | Return the artifact without the service wrapper. | The artifact is a conversation or the user requested a covering note. |
| Staged revelation / contextual | “Here's the thing”; “What if I told you”; “Plot twist”; “everything changed” | Give the news or evidence without announcing a revelation. | Suspense earns its place in a story or the supplied voice uses the device purposefully. |
| Question pivots / contextual | “The result?”; “Why does this matter?” immediately answered | Connect the ideas directly. | A real question organizes an FAQ, teaching explanation, interview, or deliberate voice. |
| Forced balance and triads / contextual | Three benefits everywhere; automatic “both sides”; equal-length sections | Use the number of items and amount of attention the material calls for. | There are actually three relevant items or a balanced comparison is the task. |
| Generic setup and transitions / contextual | “In today's rapidly evolving landscape”; “Moreover”; “When it comes to” | Begin with the topic; express the actual relationship. | Context or a transition helps readers follow a real shift. A transition word alone is harmless. |
| Decorative structure / contextual | Every paragraph gets a bold label; needless emoji; ceremonial conclusions | Keep navigation and remove furniture that interrupts the piece. | The medium, template, accessibility, scanning, or task benefits from it. |
| Performed emphasis / contextual | “Let that sink in”; “That's the real win”; repeated slogan fragments | Let the example or argument earn the emphasis. | A speech, ad, or writer sample calls for deliberate repetition or a closing line. |
| Abstract noun chains / contextual | “the implementation of the optimization of...” | Put an actor and action in the sentence when clearer. | A field's terminology is precise or the actor is genuinely unknown or irrelevant. |
| Cadence and punctuation repetition / contextual | Identical openings; equal paragraph shapes; dash chains or a march of short sentences | Revise relationships and sentence shapes where they feel mechanical. | Repetition is intentional or the meaning calls for the same construction. |

## Revisions in context

**Announcement.** “We're thrilled to announce a groundbreaking solution that
seamlessly empowers teams.” If the supplied facts are that an app now exports CSV
and exports require an admin account, use: “You can now export records as CSV.
An admin account is required.” Do not invent time savings, customers, or excitement.

**Real contrast.** “Deletion removes the file; archiving keeps it available for
restoration.” Preserve this distinction. Removing all contrast would make the
instructions less useful.

**Uncertainty.** “In the pilot, 7 of 11 participants completed the task; without a
comparison group, we cannot attribute completion to the new interface.” Keep both
the result and its limit. “The pilot proved the interface works” changes the claim.

**Already authored.** “The bus was late again. I finished the pear in my coat
pocket and walked.” If asked for a light polish, it may need no change. Do not
invent rain, frustration, or a more dramatic final sentence.

## Perishable observations

Words such as “delve,” “tapestry,” “landscape,” “pivotal,” and “seamless” have been
associated with generic model prose in the supplied research. They also have
ordinary, literal, and technical uses. These are search clues, not banned words.
Check density, precision, and fit; do not replace them merely to clear a list.

The research also describes changing punctuation and contraction patterns across
models and dates. Those empirical claims have not been independently verified in
this skill. No model-era rule is authoritative here. Do not strip dashes, add
contractions, or introduce punctuation variety as a target in itself.

When updating a volatile observation, record the date, source URL, model/domain
coverage, evidence type, limits, and exceptions. Separate corpus findings from
editorial advice and vendor marketing. Retest both the proposed fix and legitimate
uses before promoting an observation into core guidance.
