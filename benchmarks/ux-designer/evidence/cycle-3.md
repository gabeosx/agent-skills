# Cycle 3

## Hypothesis

Cycle 2 improved the opening composition in more than one family, but the dashboard lost attention to a large trend panel and the dispatch queue lost visibility to added height and repeated urgency cues. The documents and form also contain examples where supporting navigation or framing consumes space around the active content. A disciplined relationship between decision, evidence and action should make these screens easier to scan and use without mandating one visual style.

Test a focused change to the first working region, priority on small screens and the actual interaction-state inspection. Keep relevant evidence and action together, spend emphasis on the region where work happens, and retain scope and return context after the user acts. The browser loop explicitly checks a narrow exception or result before the final edit.

## Comparison

Evaluate all six frozen development tasks against Cycle 2 and the original baseline. Keep the same brief, actor grouping, grader contract and browser suite.

## Result

Rejected. Cycle 3 averaged 87.83, matching Cycle 2 in the same panel, and won three of five non-tied pairs. All six tasks passed the browser checks. Against the original baseline it gained 2.33 points and won four of six.

The lighter inspection form, clearer investigation cues in the dashboard and tighter mobile dispatch list showed that the idea can help. The losses were material to two families. The comparison lost seven points against Cycle 2 because heavy display type, tall model artwork and clipping competed with a fast reading of the values. The documentation lost six points, with weaker visual character and smaller or less legible navigation cues. Typography fell by 0.50 points on average and actor group elapsed time increased from 405 to 478 seconds. The 60% preference heuristic alone could not justify replacing Cycle 2 given these losses.
