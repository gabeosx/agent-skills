# Cycle 4

## Hypothesis

Cycles 2 and 3 show that making the layout more compact can help, but density changes alone can leave overwhelming typography or illustration in place. Cycle 3 made some core actions quicker to notice while introducing a comparison header that visually outweighed its filtered data and a documentation screen that lost clarity in its outline. Similar hierarchy conflicts appeared in earlier form progress and chart treatments.

Start from the accepted Cycle 2 source. Test a direct visual comparison during the agent's own review: mute, simplify or move a competing element and choose the result that keeps the work and identity clear. In the art-direction guidance, assign prominent visual elements a job at real size. Keep labels and values usable before spending their space on artwork. This is a critique change focused on visible contribution, different from the Cycle 3 layout and state prioritization.

## Comparison

Evaluate all six frozen tasks against Cycle 2 and the original baseline. Preserve the rejected Cycle 3 archive and trials.

## Result

Rejected. Cycle 4 scored 88.33 with a median of 89. Against Cycle 2 it declined by 0.83 points and split the six preferences. It won all six comparisons against the original baseline in the same panel, with a 5.17-point mean gain. All six sites passed the frozen browser suite.

The landing page, dashboard and dispatch queue gained relative to Cycle 2. During visual review, an implementer caught and repaired a collapsed comparison column before submission. These gains did not transfer to the rest of the set. The comparison still lost seven points relative to the leader, with less compact phone presentation and supporting artwork occupying valuable row space. The form lost three and documentation lost one. Actor group elapsed time rose from 405 to 471 seconds. Retain the tested critique idea for future research and restore the selected Cycle 2 source.

This was the second successive candidate that did not improve on the leader. Development stopped after four cycles, before opening the sealed confirmation briefs.
